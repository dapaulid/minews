## Initiative: Business warns of high costs for households

[20 Min](https://www.20min.ch/story/geld-fuer-oev-umwelt-3-000-franken-pro-haushalt-finanzierungs-zoff-um-klimafonds-103476840) • _Sunday, 28 December 2025, 18:15 AM_

A “yes” to the climate‑fund initiative in March could cost households up to CHF 3,000, warns the business sector. The initiators see long‑term benefits.

### Background of the Climate‑Fund Initiative
- Goal is a **state fund** for:
  - Expansion of renewable energies  
  - Building renovations  
  - Expansion of public transport (PT)  
  - Preservation of biodiversity  
- Financial need: annually **0.5 % to 1 % of Gross Domestic Product** (about **CHF 3.9 – 7.8 billion** according to the Federal Council, initiators estimate slightly lower).  
- The fund should be **outside the federal budget** and **outside the debt brake**.  
- The initiative is driven by the **[Social Democratic Party of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland)**, the **[Green Party (Switzerland)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_(Switzerland))**, the **[Swiss Trade Union Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Trade_Union_Federation)** and other left‑wing organisations.

### Financing debate
- **Economiesuisse** (the leading business association) warns that the initiative could lead to **costs of up to CHF 3,000 per household**.  
- Its head of location policy, **[Alexander Keberle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economiesuisse)**, argues:
  - Providing **CHF 5 – 10 billion per year** in the long term would require either radical spending cuts (three times the current austerity package) or **tax increases**.  
  - Example: raising direct federal taxes by an average of up to **CHF 3,000 per household**.  
  - These costs are **twice as high** as those of the **[Youth Initiative (Switzerland)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_Initiative_(Switzerland))** (inheritance‑tax initiative).  
- Keberle criticises the initiators for “making promises without showing the costs transparently”.

### Position of the initiators
- **Roland Fischer** (former GLP national councillor, member of the initiative committee) rejects Economiesuisse and emphasises:  
  - The initiative is **outside the debt brake** and does **not need to be financed by new taxes or austerity measures**.  
  - Public investments that create **long‑term benefit** are historically proven (e.g. the **[Gotthard Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Tunnel)** or large dams).  
- **[Elisabeth Baume‑Schneider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabeth_Baume-Schneider)**, Federal Councillor (SP), also sits on the committee but does not detail the financing strategy.  

### Political context of the vote
- On **8 March** the electorate will decide on four proposals:  
  1. **Climate‑Fund Initiative**  
  2. **SRG Halving Initiative**  
  3. **Individual Taxation Initiative**  
  4. **Cash Initiative**  
- The cost discussion is expected to dominate the debate in the coming weeks.  

### Media note
- The article comes from **20 Minutes**, which states its own guidelines aim for **ideology‑free, fact‑based reporting**.

> Relevance score: [8] – National climate‑fund initiative could raise household costs by up to CHF 3,000, affecting a large portion of the population and shaping long‑term fiscal and environmental policy.

## Switzerland pushes criminal Afghan back

[Blick](https://www.blick.ch/politik/zurueck-ins-taliban-regime-schweiz-schiebt-kriminellen-afghanen-ab-id21549698.html) • _Sunday, 28 December 2025, 11:36 AM_

Switzerland has expelled another criminal Afghan. No deportation to Syria has taken place yet.

### Recent deportations to Afghanistan
- The **[State Secretariat for Migration (SEM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Secretariat_for_Migration)** has, for over a year, requested the departure of roughly **20 sentenced Afghan citizens**; one person was deported in December.  
- **Challenge:** Switzerland has **no official diplomatic relations with the [Taliban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban) regime in [Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul)**, making deportations difficult.  
- **Failed attempt 2023:** A sentenced individual was flown from **[Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich)** via **[Istanbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul)** to Kabul, but the Taliban refused entry, and he returned to Switzerland.  

### Negotiations with the Taliban regime
- In August, **four Taliban representatives met in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva)** to coordinate future deportations.  
- During the meeting the Taliban identified **12 additional Afghan offenders**.  
- Travel costs for the Taliban delegation were covered by Switzerland.  
- SEM believes that the remaining sentenced individuals **can be expelled soon**.  

### Returns to Syria
- In parallel, SEM is discussing the deportation of criminal Syrians.  
- **Austria** deported a offender to Syria in July; **Germany** began this week with deportations to **[Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus)**.  
- A Syrian convicted in Germany of serious robbery, assault and extortion was deported to Damascus on Tuesday.  
- German Interior Minister **[Alexander Dobrindt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dobrindt)** stressed society’s interest in consistent returns.  

### Political reactions in Switzerland
- SEM points out that **cantons are responsible for executing expulsions** and that no forced returns to Syria have yet been carried out; however, they are in contact with Syrian authorities and are exploring all options.  
- The Lucerne Council of States member **[Damian Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damian_Müller)** (FDP) calls for more speed and pressure from the federal government and cantons:  
  - “If Germany can deport to Syria, Switzerland can do the same.”  
  - He expects swift action rather than mere discussion.  

> Relevance score: [7] – National immigration/deportation policy debate, touching on legal, security and human‑rights dimensions within Switzerland.

## Bankruptcy at Prime Energy: Investors lose up to 80 % of their money

[20 Min](https://www.20min.ch/story/solarfirma-am-ende-piccard-als-posterboy-glaeubiger-verlieren-bei-konkurs-fast-alles-103476878) • _Sunday, 28 December 2025, 15:36 AM_

The insolvency of Prime Energy shakes investors. Bertrand Piccard sees himself as a double victim.

### Background & bankruptcy
- Solar‑panel manufacturer **Prime Energy** from Geneva filed for bankruptcy in 2024.  
- According to the bankruptcy trustee’s estimate, **60 %–80 %** of the invested capital is lost, amounting to **over CHF 120 million**.  
- The money mainly came from the French‑speaking region and was collected from **about 2,000 private investors**.  

### Investigation & accused
- **Laurin Fäh** (company founder and main shareholder) and his former manager **Khalid Belgmimi** have been in pre‑trial detention since September.  
- Prosecutors accuse them of **misappropriation, fraud and breach of fiduciary duties** (presumption of innocence applies until a final verdict).  
- Statements from the accused:  
  - Belgmimi describes himself as a pure salesman and distances himself from the bookkeeping.  
  - Fäh says: *“If I have received the money, it is my money, and I do what I want with it.”*  

### Financial use of the funds
- The raised money was not only invested in **solar installations**, but also in:  
  - **Real estate**  
  - A **manufacturer of medical‑cannabis**  
- Fäh claims investors would have understood the situation if it had been “well explained”.  

### Role of **Bertrand Piccard**  
- The balloonist and solar‑flight pioneer **[Bertrand Piccard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_Piccard)** was for several years the public face of Prime Energy (“a bit like our Roger Federer”).  
- Piccard sees himself as a **double victim**:  
  - He invested a **six‑figure sum** himself in the company.  
  - The alleged fraud has **damaged his good reputation**.  
- He expressed outrage over the “many people who were fooled”, but admitted he **found out too late** about Prime Energy’s financial state.  

> Relevance score: [6] – Insolvency of a Swiss solar‑company affects investors and hints at risks in the domestic cleantech sector, but the fallout is limited to the financial sphere.
