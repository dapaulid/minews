## Swiss Houses Are Getting More Expensive - This Danger Threatens Owners

[Watson](https://www.watson.ch/schweiz/immobilien/798108747-schweizer-haeuser-werden-immer-teurer-diese-gefahr-droht-eigentuemern) • _Sunday, November 23, 2025, 05:36 AM_

A new analysis of 7500 real estate purchases brings worrying facts to light.

### Rising Real Estate Prices in Switzerland

- Real estate prices, especially for [[single-family homes]], have been rising for over 20 years; according to the [[Bank for International Settlements]], by 135% since 2000.
- Average single-family home costs 1.35 million [[Swiss francs]] today; wages have decoupled, normal families (e.g., the Müllers with 160,000 francs income) cannot afford it.

### Moneypark Analysis of 7500 Purchases

- [[Moneypark]] CEO [[Lukas Vogt]] analyzed purchases from the last 5 years: Many use [[pension funds]] (second pillar) to raise equity for the mortgage.
  - 2250 of 7500 buyers (every third) withdrew an average of 70% from the pension fund.
- Banks check affordability with a fictitious 5% interest rate: Costs (interest, ancillary costs, amortization) at most 1/3 of income.

### Current Low Interest Rates Enable Purchases

- Real mortgage rates below 1.5% (10 years) allow purchases; the Müller family would get a mortgage for a 1.35 million house at 2% interest.
- Pension fund withdrawal reduces the mortgage, fits the budget under the 1/3 limit.

### Risks in Retirement

- Less pension due to withdrawal → falling income; living costs (at 5% test + rising house values/ancillary costs at 1%) often exceed 1/3, possibly 40%.
- Danger: Forced house sale ("expulsion from paradise"), bank switch impossible, repayment demand.
- Affected: About 70% of buyers since 2010 with medium income; rare today, could increase.

### Moneypark Recommendations

- Save more before retirement (third pillar), pay off mortgage faster, live less paradisiacally.
- Vogt: "Financial foresight is required" to avoid overload; banks avoid forced sales, prioritize stable interest payments.

> Relevance score: [8] - Rising house prices based on 7500 transactions signal ongoing affordability crisis and potential bubble risk, structurally impacting Swiss homeowners' wealth, economy, and long-term housing market stability for a broad population.
