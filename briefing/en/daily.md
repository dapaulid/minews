## Switzerland without Vaccine Factory? Experts Warn of Repetition

[20 Min](https://www.20min.ch/story/schweiz-ohne-impfstofffabrik-experten-warnen-vor-wiederholung-103470781) • _Saturday, 27 December 2025, 15:03 AM_

Janssen’s withdrawal recalls the case of Berna Biotech. Experts fear high costs in the next pandemic.

### Background and Current Decision  
- **Janssen**, a subsidiary of **[Johnson & Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_%26_Johnson)**, plans to exit its vaccine development and move production from **[Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern)**‑Bümpliz abroad.  
- Reason:  
  * Development of a vaccine against *Klebsiella*‑induced diseases showed insufficient efficacy versus placebos.  
  * Production of **[viral vector vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_vector_vaccine)** is slated for a new plant in the Netherlands by the end of 2026.  
- As part of a “strategic review”, a legally required staff consultation procedure was carried out; it is now complete.

### Employment Consequences  
- **Up to 300 jobs** at the site could disappear.  
- Janssen is exploring options, including selling the plant to a third party.  
  * In the best case employees would be taken on; concrete sale details are still missing.  
- Affected staff will receive, according to the company:  
  * generous severance,  
  * extended notice periods (especially for those over 50),  
  * child allowances and other support measures.

### Impact on Swiss Vaccine Strategy  
- The **[Swiss Federal Office of Public Health (FOPH)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Office_of_Public_Health)** expresses regret over the relocation and points to the long tradition of the site as a vaccine‑and‑serum institute.  
- FOPH statement: Responsibility for drug‑and‑vaccine production lies primarily with the private sector; the overarching goals of the vaccine strategy are not jeopardised.  
- Over the past five years the Confederation has taken measures to strengthen the economy’s supply‑mission.

### Historical Comparison: Berna Biotech  
- **Andreas Faller** (co‑initiator of the Medical Supply Initiative) draws parallels to **Berna Biotech AG**, which previously produced vaccines at the same location.  
- In 2005 the Federal Council declined financial support for Berna Biotech; a contract for avian‑flu vaccines was moved abroad.  
- The company was later acquired by **Crucell** and then by **Johnson & Johnson**.  
- Faller argues that the missed support then cost far less than a single lockdown day (≈ 150–200 million CHF) but was regretted in the long term.

### Political Reactions and Demands  
- **Sarah Wyss** (National Councilor, **[Social Democratic Party of Switzerland (SP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland)**) stresses the importance of local production sites for supply security, especially after the **[COVID‑19 pandemic in Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Switzerland)**.  
  * She calls for better framework conditions, investment in research, strong universities and the further development of a national pharma strategy (e.g., purchase contracts).  
- **Rémy Wyssmann** (National Councilor, **[Swiss People’s Party (SVP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_People%27s_Party)**) sees the departure as a result of an increasingly investment‑unfriendly Switzerland and points to the cost advantages of overseas production.  

### Proposals for Future Measures  
- Establish clear **vaccine strategies** and targeted incentives to keep research, development and production in the country.  
- Possible instruments:  
  * State contributions to **research‑and‑development projects**,  
  * Participation in production facilities through **[Public‑Private Partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public%E2%80%93private_partnership)**.  
- Goal: Ensure that key production capacities remain at least in Europe.

> Relevance score: [7] – Potential loss of a domestic vaccine production site raises significant concerns for Swiss health security and future pandemic preparedness.

## Behind Keller‑Sutter Lies a Difficult Year

[Swiss Radio and Television (SRF)](https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/1-jahr-bundespraesidentin-kein-schreckliches-aber-ein-schwieriges-jahr-fuer-keller-sutter) • _Saturday, 27 December 2025, 14:11 AM_

The customs dispute with the United States shaped Karin Keller‑Sutter’s year in office. In the Federal Council she remains a power player.

### Customs Dispute with the USA  
- During her presidential year **[Karin Keller‑Sutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karin_Keller%E2%80%93Sutter)** fell into a serious customs conflict after a botched phone call with **[Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump)**.  
- Instead of the contractually intended 10 % tariff, Switzerland was hit with a punitive duty of 39 %.  
- Keller‑Sutter called the outcome a “disappointment of the Federal Council”, because the negotiated agreement had already been approved in July.

### Political Assessment  
- **[Adrian Vatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Vatter)** (University of Bern) finds the labels “puppet‑master” or “failed” exaggerated:  
  - The media hype after the initial call (April) and the temporary suspension of duties was overblown.  
  - He reads the communication errors more as a symptom of highly emotionalised and personalised politics.  
- Despite criticism, Keller‑Sutter is still viewed in the **[Federal Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Council_(Switzerland))** as an important power factor.

### Role in the Federal Council  
- **[Thierry Burkart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thierry_Burkart)** (FDP) praises Keller‑Sutter for making the Council appear more united under her presidency.  
- The former FDP president highlights her successful navigation of a difficult geopolitical situation, giving the overall outcome a positive sheen.

### Financial‑Policy Positions  
- Keller‑Sutter stays firm on **[UBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UBS_(bank))**, pushing for stricter rules for large banks despite heavy lobbying.  
- In fiscal policy she consistently defends the **debt brake** and a **relief package**.  
- **Sarah Wyss** (SP) acknowledges her perseverance on the UBS issue but criticises the “panic‑making” she engaged in concerning the federal budget, resulting in a mixed record.

### Conclusion  
- Karin Keller‑Sutter’s presidential year was not catastrophic but clearly difficult, marked by international trade conflicts, political criticism and active fiscal initiatives.

> Relevance score: [7] – The customs dispute with the United States (39 % tariff vs. 10 % expected) directly affects Swiss trade policy and the Federal Council’s standing.

## Geneva Overtakes Zurich 2026 in Fiscal Equalisation

[Swiss Radio and Television (SRF)](https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/noch-vor-zug-und-zuerich-darum-zahlt-genf-2026-am-meisten-in-den-finanzausgleich-ein) • _Saturday, 27 December 2025, 06:57 AM_

The western canton is for the first time the biggest contributor. With CHF 543 million it stays ahead of Zug and Zurich.

### Result of the 2026 Fiscal Equalisation  
- **Geneva** is for the first time the largest payer in the **National Fiscal Equalisation (NFA)** and must contribute **CHF 543 million** in 2026.  
- This puts Geneva ahead of the previous leaders **Zurich** and **Zug**.

### Causes and Financial Consequences for Geneva  
- Finance Director **Nathalie Fontanet** explains that the rise stems from high **tax revenues** in 2022.  
  - The main driver was **raw‑material trading** during the price‑ and supply‑crisis, which poured huge revenues into the state coffers.  
- With commodity prices falling since 2022, revenues are now declining, so the retroactive NFA calculation arrives now.  
- Fontanet plans an extensive **savings programme** for next spring to cover the additional canton‑budget outlays.

### Political Reactions and New Role  
- **Carlo Sommaruga** (SP Council of States) stresses that the higher contribution does not automatically translate into more political weight in **Bern**:  
  - Cantons benefitting from the equalisation note that they co‑finance Geneva’s prosperity (e.g., the **watch manufacturers** in the **Jura**).  
  - Historical prejudices (“Western Switzerland as the Greeks of Switzerland”) are long gone – today Geneva is the “top‑class”.  
- **Mauro Poggia** (Mouvement Citoyen Genevois) sees the payments to the Confederation as an **investment** in Geneva’s international standing, especially the **UN headquarters**:  
  - “When Geneva does well, Switzerland does well too.”

### Outlook  
- The Swiss federal finance administration forecasts that Geneva will continue to climb the **resource index** and remain the biggest NFA payer for the next four years.  
- This development brings both **opportunity** (strengthening the international role) and **challenging fiscal tightening** for Geneva’s own budget.

> Relevance score: [7] – Geneva becoming the top net contributor in the cantonal fiscal equalisation system reshapes Swiss inter‑cantonal finances, a notable national development.

## Strong Earthquake Shakes Taiwan

[blue News](https://www.bluewin.ch/de/news/vermischtes/schweres-erdbeben-vor-der-kueste-taiwans-3025673.html) • _Saturday, 27 December 2025, 17:33 AM_

A strong quake struck off Taiwan’s coast. The tremor, about 30 km east of Yilan County, was recorded at magnitude 7.0, the Taiwanese weather agency said. It occurred around 23:00 local time at a depth of over 72 km. No further details were initially available.

### Event  

- A strong earthquake of magnitude **Mw 7.0** shook the east coast of **[Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan)** around 23:00 local time.  
- The epicentre was about **30 km east of **[Yilan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yilan_County)** at a depth of over **72 km**.  
- The magnitude was confirmed by Taiwan’s **[Central Weather Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Weather_Bureau_(Taiwan))**.

### Impact  

- According to the **[Central News Agency (CNA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_News_Agency)**, there were initially no reports of injuries or major damage.  
- The quake was felt in northern and eastern Taiwan with intensity 4.0; it was also noted in the major cities **Taipei** and **Hsinchu**.  
- Weather‑agency experts warned of **significant aftershocks** in the coming days.

### Historical Context  

- The quake is the **third‑strongest** in recent Taiwanese history after the events of **1999** and **2024**.  
- Taiwan sits on the **[Ring of Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_Fire)**, a horseshoe‑shaped zone that frequently experiences strong earthquakes and volcanic eruptions.

### Further Reports  

- The event was also broadcast on **[TVBS World Taiwan](https://twitter.com/tvbsworldtaiwan)** on Twitter with live updates.  
- Shortly after the quake, videos from the affected areas were shared on social media.

> Relevance score: [6] – A magnitude‑7 earthquake in Taiwan poses a humanitarian crisis and could disrupt global supply chains (semiconductors), which indirectly affects Switzerland.

## President Announces Major Expansion

[Blick](https://www.blick.ch/wirtschaft/nach-umbau-folgt-der-grosse-ausbau-migros-praesidentin-kuendigt-140-filialen-und-1000-neue-mitarbeitende-an-id21551272.html) • _Saturday, 27 December 2025, 11:18 AM_

It is the biggest expansion in such a short time, says Migros President Ursula Nold. The new stores will often be smaller.

### Background and Previous Changes  
- **[Migros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migros)** has carried out its largest transformation in recent years:  
  - Separation of specialist formats such as **Do it + Garden**, **SportX**, **Melectronics** and **Micasa**.  
  - Sale of **Hotelplan**.  
  - **1 300 employees** were affected by the changes.  
- **Ursula Nold** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursula_Nold)), the company’s president (56 years), says the “big changes have now been completed”.

### New Expansion Plan to 2029  
- Goal: **140 new outlets** by **2029** – the biggest rollout in such a short period.  
- Migros plans to hire **over 1 000 new employees each year**.  
- Currently about **1 000 vacancies** and **850 open apprenticeship positions** remain to be filled.

### Labour Market & Skills Shortages  
- Migros especially feels bottlenecks in:  
  - **Bakers** and **butchers** (craft trades).  
  - **Truck drivers**.  
  - Technical professions in general.  
- These gaps make the planned expansion harder and require targeted recruitment.

### Strategy of Smaller Outlets  
- The new shops will be **smaller** than usual.  
- Reasons:  
  - **Proximity to people** in residential neighborhoods, to reach roughly **200 000 additional households**.  
  - Change in **shopping behaviour**: customers shop less in bulk and more frequently and purposefully, often after work.  
- Smaller formats should be more flexible to these evolving needs.

> Relevance score: [6] – Migros’s largest restructuring impacts a major Swiss employer and the retail sector, with noticeable economic and labour implications.

## Army Chief Thomas Süssli Emphasises: Neutrality Needs Defence

[20 Min](https://www.20min.ch/story/abtretender-armeechef-neutralitaet-hat-nur-dann-wert-wenn-man-sie-verteidigen-kann-103476399) • _Saturday, 27 December 2025, 10:34 AM_

Reflecting on his tenure, Thomas Süssli talks about Swiss defence capability and the threat from Russia.

### Background and Context  
- **Thomas Süssli** is ending a six‑year term as head of the **[Swiss Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Armed_Forces)**; **Benedikt Roos** will succeed him at year‑end.  
- His term coincided with the outbreak of the **[2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine)**, and Russia remains a strategic threat to Europe.  
- Süssli stresses that Swiss **[neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_neutrality)** only endures if backed by effective defence forces.

### Key Points from Thomas Süssli  
1. **Possible Russian Expansion**  
   - European defence ministers and army chiefs warn Moscow may seek to widen the war from **2028** onward.  
   - Süssli sees no concrete date for an open NATO‑Russia conflict but calls for preventive steps to avoid escalation.  

2. **Russia’s Goal of Great‑Power Status**  
   - The Kremlin aims for geopolitical influence, e.g., unifying East‑Slavic peoples or destabilising Europe.  
   - **Cyber‑attacks**, disinformation and espionage are already active in Switzerland.  

3. **Limits of the Army in Large‑Scale Attacks**  
   - Below war‑threshold incidents the army can act mainly as a subsidiary to cantons.  
   - It cannot repel **“threats from a distance”** or a comprehensive assault on Switzerland.  

4. **Equipment Deficits**  
   - In a crisis only about **one‑third** of soldiers would be fully equipped – a strain for Süssli.  
   - Funding responsibility lies with the democratically elected state, not the military.  

5. **Lack of Societal Push**  
   - Despite the Ukraine war, there has been no significant shift in public awareness or openness in Switzerland.  
   - Reasons: a **180‑year peace** period, geographic distance, and the assumption that neutrality automatically protects.  

6. **Neutrality Must Be Defended**  
   - Historically, neutral states that remained undefended have been drawn into wars.  
   - Neutrality only has value when underpinned by **weapons** and defence capability.  

7. **Innovation Pressure**  
   - Süssli demands: **“Innovate or die.”**  
   - The army is building a system where militia soldiers can contribute ideas; digital processes must be exponentially scalable.  
   - This cultural shift is likened to private‑sector change and will take years.  

8. **Need for International Cooperation**  
   - Switzerland cannot defend itself alone; it must be **interoperable** with other armies.  
   - Preparations for such partnerships are already underway.

### Outlook and Challenges  
- **Strengthening defence**: improve equipment, expand digital innovation and raise readiness.  
- **Deepening international cooperation**: interoperability with NATO and EU partners to rely on allies in a crisis.  
- **Societal discourse**: promote a realistic understanding of neutrality and the need for a robust defence to meet future threats.

> Relevance score: [6] – The army chief’s remarks on defence readiness address core Swiss security policy amid heightened geopolitical tensions.

## Federal Councilor Martin Pfister on Fighter Jets and the State of the Army

[srf.ch](https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/gespraech-zum-jahresende-wie-viel-zug-steckt-noch-in-ihnen-herr-bundesrat-martin-pfister) • _Saturday, 27 December 2025, 09:04 AM_

Martin Pfister has been in office for ten months. In the interview he explains what has moved him during his early tenure as a Federal Councilor.

### Background and Political Career  
- **[Martin Pfister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Pfister)** (born 1963 in **[Zug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zug_(canton))**) was unexpectedly elected to the **[Federal Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Council_(Switzerland))** in March 2025.  
- Before his election he served from 2016 to 2025 as a member of the Zug cantonal government and health minister.  
- He studied history and German studies, is a trained teacher, and was a colonel with eight years leading disaster‑relief operations.

### Role in the Federal Council and Regional Representation  
- Pfister says his entry was “like an avalanche” and that for over 20 years he has represented Central Switzerland in the Federal Palace.  
- His origin from **Zug** gives him “a special connection” to representatives of the central cantons in parliament and allows direct contact with their governments.  
- While he stresses he must act in the national interest, his regional roots help him understand local issues such as the **Luzern underground station** or the **Zimmerberg tunnel**.

### Defence Policy and F‑35 Fighter‑Jet Procurement  
- In the Department of Defence, Civil Protection and Sports (DDPS) Pfister is responsible for the **[F‑35 Lightning II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_F-35_Lightning_II)** purchase.  
- The popular mandate‑approved budget of CHF 6 billion is insufficient to fund the planned 36 aircraft; Pfister therefore triggered a supplemental credit.  
- He stresses the difficult security situation in Europe and the need to equip the **[Swiss Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Armed_Forces)** adequately.

### Problems of the Swiss Army  
- Pfister identifies two main gaps: **equipment** and **ammunition**.  
- Despite motivated young soldiers and good training, the existing materiel is usable but lacking in quantity and ammo.  
- He plans to close these gaps in the coming years to ensure a “strong army”.

### Sporting Activities in Office  
- Pfister admits he rarely finds time for sport.  
- His secretary now jogs with him once a week whenever the schedule permits.

*Interview conducted by **Sämi Studer**.*

> Relevance score: [6] – Discussion of fighter‑jet procurement and army condition is directly relevant to Swiss defence planning and budgeting.
