## SNB monetary policy: Will rates stay at zero or fall into negative territory?

[20 Minutes](https://www.20min.ch/story/oekonomen-legen-sich-fest-nullzinsen-oder-minuskurs-so-entscheidet-der-snb-praesident-103467354) • *Wednesday, December 10, 2025, 7:45 PM*

The Swiss National Bank faces a decision. What does this mean for mortgages and the economy?

### Background & expectations survey  
- On **Thursday**, **[Martin Schlegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Schlegel)**, as President of the **[Swiss National Bank (SNB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_National_Bank)**, announced the upcoming policy‑rate decision.  
- A survey carried out by **[Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/)** of 40 economists shows:  
  - **38 experts** expect the policy rate to remain at **0 %**.  
  - **2 experts** see a possible cut to **‑0.25 %**.  

### Scenario 1 – Maintaining zero rates  
- **Inflation & growth**: In November inflation fell by 0.2 % (month‑on‑month); GDP growth is around 0.5 %. According to **Philipp Burckhardt** (Fixed‑Income Strategist, Lombard Odier) this is not enough to push rates into negative territory.  
- **Impacts**:  
  - **Mortgage rates**: Market has already priced in the scenario → little reaction.  
  - **Rent prices**: The mortgage reference rate stays under pressure, but demand for rental housing remains the main driver.  
  - **Inflation**: Remains low in the short term, likely to rise again in the medium term.  
  - **Bank deposits**: No interest, negative rates remain a “theoretical scenario”.  
  - **Euro/Swiss‑franc exchange rate**: Because of the rate differential with the **[European Central Bank (ECB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Central_Bank)** (policy rate about 2 %), the franc stays unattractive → long‑term depreciation against the euro.  
  - **Economy**: Zero rates boost credit demand and thus investment, supporting the Swiss economy.  

### Scenario 2 – Cutting to **‑0.25 %**  
- **Market reaction**: A negative rate would surprise markets, as the environment (high EU rates) rather suggests a pause.  
- **Impacts**:  
  - **Mortgage rates**: Decline, as the scenario was not expected.  
  - **Rent prices**: Further pressure on the reference rate, but strong rental demand stays decisive.  
  - **Inflation**: Low as in the first scenario, but medium‑term upward pressure remains.  
  - **Bank deposits**: Banks face greater pressure and could pass negative rates more aggressively onto customers.  
  - **Euro/Swiss‑franc exchange rate**: The large rate differential with the ECB keeps the franc unattractive → further depreciation against the euro.  
  - **Economy**: Even lower borrowing costs raise investment demand, stimulating growth.  

### Implications for consumers & businesses  
- **Savvy & pension funds**: Remain at zero rates with no return; with negative rates they might face fees or deductions.  
- **Borrowers**: Benefit from low or falling mortgage and consumer loan rates.  
- **Export industry**: A weaker franc supports competitiveness.  
- **Tourism (shopping tourists)**: Also benefit from a cheaper franc.  

---  
*The final SNB decision will largely determine whether the current policy continues to stimulate the economy or adds further strain on savers and the financial system.*

> Relevance score: [9] – Decisions of the Swiss National Bank directly affect mortgage rates, credit conditions and the broader economy. High relevance for all Swiss residents.

## Up to 250 million extra costs: Bern station expansion appears to take significantly longer and cost much more

[blue News](https://www.bluewin.ch/de/news/schweiz/ausbau-des-berner-bahnhofs-dauert-offenbar-deutlich-laenger-als-geplant-3004416.html) • *Thursday, December 11, 2025, 5:47 AM*

The upgraded Bern railway station is now expected to open only in 2031. Last year the target was end‑2029. The construction works are not only delayed but also far more expensive.

### Project overview  
- The **[Bern railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern_railway_station)** is now expected to open in **2031**, instead of the **end‑2029** date that was still planned in 2022.  
- The rebuild now costs about **40 % more** than originally estimated: an additional **CHF 200‑250 million**.  

### Reasons for the delays  
- **Objections** from residents and interest groups have repeatedly pushed the timetable back.  
- **Geological difficulties** and complex construction work, e.g.:  
  - **Contaminated sites** on the building ground ([contaminated site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contaminated_site))  
  - **Poor anchoring** and required **ground freezing**  
- **Pandemic‑related** effects (COVID‑19 pandemic) caused supply shortages and labor gaps ([COVID‑19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic)).  

### Development of the cost framework  
- Original budget: **CHF 974 million** for the rebuild, including a new pedestrian underpass and a new **[Regionalverkehr Bern–Solothurn underground station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regionalverkehr_Bern–Solothurn)**.  
- The increased spending stems from the technical and planning problems mentioned above, as well as higher material and labor costs.  

### Conclusion  
- The station expansion is significantly behind schedule and over budget.  
- The extra **CHF 200‑250 million** and the new 2031 opening date illustrate how complex large‑scale infrastructure projects in urban areas can become.

## Solar initiative: All suitable roofs and façades should receive solar panels

[Swiss Radio and Television](https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/solar-installationspflicht-solar-initiative-mit-134-000-unterschriften-eingereicht) • *Wednesday, December 10, 2025, 8:53 PM*

The initiative aims to equip all suitable roofs and façades in Switzerland with solar panels.

### Background  
- The **[The Greens (Switzerland)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Greens_(Switzerland))** have submitted **134 000 signatures** for the **Solar Initiative** to the **[Federal Chancellery (Switzerland)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Chancellery_(Switzerland))**.  
- The goal is to equip all suitable roofs and façades in **Switzerland** with **solar modules**.  

### Core points of the initiative  
- **Official title:** “For a secure supply of renewable energy (Solar Initiative)”.  
- **Obligation to install** solar panels on roofs and façades, except when:  
  - Protective interests (e.g., heritage protection, nature and species protection) prevail, **or**  
  - Installation would be disproportionate for other reasons.  

### Political argumentation  
- Party president **[Lisa Mazzone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Mazzone)** stresses that the initiative simultaneously strengthens climate and nature protection and advances the **energy transition** without having to rely on expensive and risky **[nuclear power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power)** plants.  
- The Greens argue that Switzerland could take on more responsibility domestically by increasing solar generation and would not need to “buy” climate offsets abroad.  

### Procedural status  
- A popular vote requires at least **100 000 valid signatures**; the Federal Chancellery is reviewing the validity of the submitted signatures.  
- This would be the first **[popular initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_initiative_(Switzerland))** in ten years in which the Greens collected all signatures themselves.  

> Relevance score: [8] – National political initiative aiming to mandate solar panel installation on all suitable Swiss roofs and façades. Could reshape the energy mix, climate policy and construction sector.

## Bloomberg reports: Massive job cuts at Pfizer Switzerland

[Blick](https://www.blick.ch/wirtschaft/laut-bericht-pfizer-streicht-hunderte-stellen-in-der-schweiz-id21505847.html) • *Wednesday, December 10, 2025, 6:40 PM*

Pfizer plans, according to a report, a massive lay‑off in Switzerland. The pharma company wants to reduce its Swiss workforce from 300 to about 70 employees by year‑end. The move is part of a multi‑year cost‑reduction programme.

### Background  
- **Pfizer** is carrying out a multi‑year **[cost‑reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_reduction)** programme of roughly **CHF 5.6 billion** through 2027.  
- The company is scaling back its presence in **[Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland)**, one of the country’s key export sectors.  

### Swiss job cuts  
- The Swiss subsidiary’s staff will be reduced from about **300** to **≈ 70** employees by the end of 2023.  
- The cuts involve several hundred positions and are part of the broader cost‑reduction strategy.  

### Leadership change and limited scope  
- **Sabine Bruckner**, long‑time head of the Swiss business, will move on 1 December to Pfizer’s international team, taking on a global role.  
- **Res Lal** will take over the Swiss business, but with a **limited mandate**, according to Bloomberg sources.  

### Industry context  
- **Novartis** announced in November that, because of an **automation offensive**, it would cut about **550** jobs in Switzerland.  
- The Pfizer reductions therefore follow a trend where large pharma giants are reshaping their cost structures in Switzerland.
