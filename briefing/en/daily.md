## Court Allows Climate Lawsuit Against Holcim

[Swiss Radio and Television](https://www.srf.ch/news/wirtschaft/insulaner-gegen-zementkonzern-gericht-laesst-klimaklage-zu-muss-holcim-fuer-klimaschaeden-zahlen) • *Monday, December 22, 2025, 06:00 Uhr*

For the first time in Switzerland, victims are allowed to sue a corporation for climate damage. Holcim has filed an appeal.

### Background of the lawsuit
- Four residents of the Indonesian island **[Pari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia)** are suing the **Zug Cantonal Court** ([Cantonal Court of Zug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonal_Court_of_Zug)) against the cement giant **[Holcim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holcim)**.  
- They hold Holcim partly responsible for climate change that raises sea levels and threatens the island’s existence.  
- The plaintiffs demand that Holcim accelerate its CO₂‑reduction and assume part of the costs generated by global warming.

### Facts about Holcim and its emissions
- Holcim belongs to the **“Carbon Majors”** ([Carbon majors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_majors)), a group of 180 companies responsible for the bulk of anthropogenic CO₂ emissions worldwide.  
- Since 1950 the company has emitted roughly twice as much CO₂ as Switzerland.  
- According to its own figures, emissions have been cut by more than 50 % since 2015; the firm aims to produce climate‑neutral cement by 2050.

### Court ruling
- The Zug Cantonal Court declares the lawsuit **admissible** – a first in Switzerland.  
- The judgment does not yet decide whether Holcim is actually liable for climate damage on Pari.  
- Holcim has already announced an appeal.

### Holcim’s position
- Holcim argues that courts are not the proper venue for climate protection; that is the job of politics.  
- The court points out that judicial decisions **do not replace** democratically legitimised climate policy, but **complement** it.  
- This case concerns concrete demands of a small island, not Swiss climate policy as a whole.

### Further proceedings
- **Next instance:** the **Zug Court of Appeals**.  
  - The parties can first bring the judgment to the **Federal Supreme Court** in Lausanne and later to the **European Court of Human Rights** ([European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights)).  
- Only after a final admissibility can issues of compensation and stricter CO₂ measures be clarified; the process is expected to take years.

### Possible consequences
- Confirmation of admissibility could increase pressure on large CO₂ emitters worldwide.  
- Companies face a choice:  
  1. Accelerate emission cuts and reshape business models.  
  2. Apply political pressure or block courts to prevent future lawsuits.

### International significance
- The ruling fits into a global trend: about 60 countries host almost 3 000 ongoing climate lawsuits.  
- The **[International Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Court_of_Justice)** has recently recognised climate protection as a human right.  
- A German court recently admitted a climate lawsuit by a Peruvian farmer against the energy conglomerate **[RWE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RWE)**.  
- The Swiss decision is therefore being watched worldwide.

## Peace Talks in Miami: Ukraine Positive

U.S. and Ukrainian representatives discussed a possible end to the war in Miami. President Zelenskyy expressed satisfaction with the talks. U.S. negotiator Witkoff sees a willingness for peace on both sides. However, a major breakthrough did not materialise.

### Course of the Miami talks
- Representatives of the **USA** and the **Ukraine** met in **Miami**, Florida, to discuss a possible end to the **Ukraine war**.  
- **[Volodymyr Zelenskyy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volodymyr_Zelenskyy)** said he was satisfied with the atmosphere and stressed ongoing work on  
  - documents for ending the war,  
  - security guarantees,  
  - reconstruction plans.  
- U.S. negotiator **Steve Witkoff** also expressed satisfaction and referred to talks on  
  - multilateral security guarantees,  
  - economic development of territories occupied by Russia since 2022.  
- In addition to the USA and Ukraine, **European partners** took part in the negotiations.

### Participants and their positions
- **USA**  
  - Emphasises constructive dialogue and a positive mood.  
  – Refers to the peace plan presented in November and the willingness to provide multilateral security guarantees.  
- **Ukraine**  
  - Commits to a “just and lasting peace”.  
  - Awaits detailed reports from the negotiation team, including possible timelines for decisions.  
- **Russia / Kremlin**  
  - **[Kirill Dmitriev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirill_Dmitriev)** was to gather information on the revised peace plan in Miami but brought no new message from **[Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Putin)**.  
  - The Kremlin publicly dampened expectations of a swift peace and called the known plan details “not constructive”.

### Main topics of the negotiations
- **Security guarantees** – multilateral commitments, likely involving the **USA**, **NATO**, and other partners.  
- **Reconstruction and economic development** – strategies for the war‑torn regions of Ukraine.  
- **Timetable** – discussion of possible deadlines for concrete decisions and implementation steps.  
- **Territorial issues** – speculation that Ukraine might consider ceding territory in exchange for security guarantees – a point Russia is expected to reject.

### Outlook and open questions
- **No major breakthrough**: despite a constructive atmosphere, no concrete outcome acceptable to all sides has yet emerged.  
- **Russia’s stance**: without clear directives from Moscow, it remains uncertain whether the proposed revisions to the peace plan will be accepted by the Kremlin.  
- **Next steps**: Ukraine expects detailed reports from its negotiation team, while the USA and its allies continue working on a multilateral peace framework.

## Migros Splits Its Employees into Two Classes for Pension Provision

[Nau.ch](https://www.nau.ch/news/schweiz/migros-teilt-ihre-angestellten-bei-der-vorsorge-in-zwei-klassen-67078363) • *Sunday, December 21, 2025, 20:10 Uhr*

Migros is renowned for its attractive pension‑fund conditions. Yet not all employees of the cooperative benefit from them.

### Background and total staff count
- The **[Migros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migros)** employs roughly **86 600 people** nationwide.  
- The company is known not only as a retail giant but also for the **attractive conditions** of its **Migros pension fund (MPK)**.

### Benefits of the Migros pension fund
- **13th month pension** for retirees in **2022**.  
- **5 % flat pension increase** in **2023**.  
- **7.5 % interest** on the savings capital in **2024** (far above the statutory minimum of **1.25 %**).  
- **CHF 7,500 bonus** for MPK members in **2024**.  
- Guaranteed **savings interest of 7.25 %** from **2026** onward.

### Unequal treatment and affected subsidiaries
- Employees of subsidiaries do **not** receive the same MPK benefits, e.g.:  
  - **[Denner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denner_(supermarket))**  
  - **[Digitec Galaxus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digitec_Galaxus)**  
  - **Medbase**  
  - **Molino‑Pizzerien**  
- Although invited to the **100‑year celebration** in Glarus, staff of these companies enjoy **significantly poorer pension conditions**.

### Insurance coverage at year‑end 2024
- **Just over half** of Migros employees in Switzerland were **insured with the MPK**.  
- The remainder is covered by:  
  - the **community foundation for Migros subsidiaries**,  
  - the pension scheme **Globo M**,  
  - **external pension funds**.

### Differences to the pension schemes of the subsidiaries
- **Globo M** (pension scheme for subsidiaries) offers:  
  - **No regular pension increases**.  
  - **Lower contributions** and overall **poorer benefits** compared with the MPK.

### Historical justification by Migros
- **Migros spokesperson Andy Zesiger** explained to the **[Neue Zürcher Zeitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neue_Zürcher_Zeitung)**:  
  - Companies that were part of the Migros group when the **[Occupational Pension Act (BVG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_Pension_Act)** came into force in **1985** are **mostly insured with the MPK**.  
  - **Later‑joined companies** are **often not** in the MPK and instead use other pension arrangements.

---  

*The summary shows that the attractive MPK benefits are not applied uniformly across all Migros‑group employees, but are historically limited mainly to the older business units.*
